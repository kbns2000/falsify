Say what you believe about the market.
An AI will try to prove it wrong.

Falsify turns your belief into one exact rule, seals it before looking at any result, replays it on historical candles, then runs a battery of attacks: random entries, fees, time splits, nearby parameters, other assets, and a devil's advocate powered by Meta's open-weight Muse Glimmer. It never tells you what to buy.

How a claim is tried

  1. Understand. The model rewrites your words as one testable rule in a tiny, safe condition language and lists every assumption it had to make. It may repair its own mistakes; it is forbidden from tuning the rule.
  2. Seal. The rule is hashed and chained to the public ledger before any result exists. No moving the goalposts afterwards.
  3. Test. A deterministic engine replays the rule: signal at the close, entry at the next open, fees on both sides. No model touches the numbers.
  4. Attack. Random entries with the same holding period, fee sensitivity, first half vs second half, nearby parameters, other assets, multiple-testing correction, sample size.
  5. Advocate. The model proposes extra counter-tests specific to your belief: the mirror-image trade, a different horizon, the asset you actually trade.
  6. Verdict. FALSIFIED, FRAGILE, SURVIVED (for now) or INCONCLUSIVE, decided by the numbers, explained by the model in your language.
